## Structuring a compliant referral arrangement

**This is not legal advice. Engage securities counsel before any outreach program or any payment.**

1. **Finder / referral fees are the main risk.** Paying anyone transaction-based compensation (a percentage of capital raised, a per-investor fee) for introducing investors to a securities offering can require the recipient to be a registered broker-dealer (Exchange Act Section 15(a)) and may implicate state law (California). Paying unregistered persons can give investors rescission rights and put the offering exemption at risk. Do not offer or imply such payments until counsel confirms a structure.
2. **Safer starting points (confirm with counsel):** (a) no-compensation relationship referrals, where the partner only shares your information; (b) use of a registered broker-dealer or placement agent under a written agreement; (c) flat, non-contingent fees for genuine non-securities services (e.g., sponsorship of an educational event at fair market value), reviewed by counsel.
3. **RIAs and CPAs have their own constraints:** investment advisers owe fiduciary duties and must disclose conflicts; many firms and licensing bodies prohibit or require disclosure of referral compensation; CPAs have commission/referral-fee rules under the California Board of Accountancy and AICPA standards. Attorneys have State Bar rules on fee-sharing and referrals.
4. **Rule 506(c) specifics:** general solicitation is permitted but the issuer must take reasonable steps to verify each investor's accredited status (e.g., tax returns, bank/brokerage statements, or a third-party letter from a registered broker-dealer, RIA, attorney or CPA). Partners must not pre-qualify investors on your behalf unless counsel approves the process. File Form D and check California notice filings.
5. **Disclosure and messaging:** all materials must be accurate and not misleading. Describe the ~15% annual return only as a targeted, not guaranteed, return; disclose that receivables are paid only when insurers or settlements pay, timing is uncertain, and principal may be lost; describe the 3:1 collateral accurately and its limits. Partners must use only approved materials.
6. **Paper it:** written referral/non-solicitation or introduction agreement, partner conflict disclosures, no investment advice from partners, record of who was introduced, and a compliance review of any events or webinars. Consider bad-actor (Rule 506(d)) diligence on covered persons and any compensated solicitors.
7. **Patient and PI-attorney channels:** do not target patients, and avoid anything that looks like influencing medical or legal referrals (anti-kickback and fee-splitting laws, Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code and federal health-care laws). Have healthcare counsel review the receivables program's relationship to provider referrals.
