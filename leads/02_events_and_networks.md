## Rules to follow at every event (506(c) checklist)

1. **Public promotion is allowed, but every purchaser must be verified.** Rule 506(c) permits general solicitation (speaking, sponsoring, ads, podcasts, social) only if PFD takes "reasonable steps to verify" each purchaser's accredited status. A checked box or self-attestation is not enough. Source: [SEC - Rule 506(c)](https://sec.gov/education/smallbusiness/exemptofferings/rule506c).
2. **The $100k minimum does not by itself satisfy the verification shortcut.** March 2025 SEC staff guidance lets issuers rely on a high minimum investment plus written representations, but the guidance cites at least $200k for natural persons and $1M for entities. At $100k, plan on documentary or third-party verification (CPA/attorney/RIA/broker-dealer letter, tax returns, W-2s, bank/brokerage statements). Sources: [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/new-sec-guidance-eases-burden-in-rule-506c-accredited-investor-verification-requirements), [Cozen](https://cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2025/sec-staff-releases-updated-guidance-on-private-offerings-involving-general-solicitation). Confirm current guidance with counsel.
3. **No guarantees.** Use "targeted" return language only, with risk disclosure (illiquidity, PI case outcome/collection risk, concentration, loss of principal possible). 3:1 collateral reduces but does not remove risk. Do not show past performance without full context and counsel review.
4. **Do not pay unregistered finders transaction-based fees.** Event hosts, podcasters, newsletter owners or club leaders who are paid per investor raised can create unregistered broker-dealer problems. Flat-fee advertising/sponsorship is the safer structure; anything success-based goes through counsel or a registered broker-dealer.
5. **Offer legends and Form D.** Use required disclaimers on all materials (not an offer to non-accredited investors; securities not registered; risk language). File Form D after first sale and handle California notice filing and fee; confirm "bad actor" (Rule 506(d)) diligence on all covered persons.
6. **Do not take subscriptions, money or documents at events.** Collect contact info only; move interested people into a gated process (accreditation verification, then data room/PPM).
7. **Keep investor and provider messaging separate.** At physician/medical-society events, the audience may be potential provider-clients, not investors. Never share patient information; keep provider-acquisition and investor-solicitation materials distinct.
8. **Conference-specific limits.** Some forums (invitation-only, RIA-only, family-office-only) restrict sponsors or prohibit direct solicitation of attendees. Read exhibitor/sponsor terms. RIAs and family offices have their own fiduciary and due-diligence processes; expect 3-9 month diligence cycles.
9. **Record keeping.** Log every lead's source, what they were shown, and date; keep it for the verification file.
