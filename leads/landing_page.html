How it works

  1. Doctors treat. Personal-injury physicians treat patients whose bills are payable from an insurance claim or settlement, which can take a long time.
  2. Investors fund. Pooled investor capital funds those medical receivables, so the practice gets working capital sooner.
  3. Collateral. Funding is structured with a target collateral ratio of 3:1 against the amount advanced. Collateral value is an estimate and may be less than expected.
  4. Repayment. Receivables are repaid as insurers or settlements pay, and proceeds are distributed per the offering documents.

Important risk disclosure

This investment is speculative and illiquid. You could lose some or all of your principal. Please consider, among others, these risks:

  • Delayed settlements. Personal-injury cases can take years to resolve. Payment timing is uncertain and delays reduce or defer returns.
  • Case losses. If a patient’s case is lost, dismissed, or settles for less than the medical bills, the receivable may be reduced or not paid at all.
  • Collateral valuation. Collateral (including receivables and claims) is hard to value, may be disputed, and may be worth materially less than stated when collected or sold.
  • Illiquidity. There is no public market. Interests are restricted securities with limited or no ability to transfer or redeem. You must be able to hold for the full term.
  • Loss of principal. Returns are not guaranteed, and no government agency insures this investment.
  • Counterparty, concentration, and legal/regulatory risk. Results depend on a limited number of physicians, attorneys, and payors, and on changes in law, lien and fee regulations, and court rulings.
  • Past results do not predict future results. Any target return is a goal and not a prediction or promise.
