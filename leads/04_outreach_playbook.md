## 0. Global rules (read before using any asset)

**Words and claims**
1. Never use: "guaranteed", "risk-free", "safe", "fixed" (as in fixed return), "secure return", "can't lose", "no risk", "assured", "insured" (unless counsel confirms a specific policy), "FDIC-like", "bank-like".
2. Always say "targeted" or "target" next to any return figure, and say "not guaranteed; investors can lose some or all of their investment" at least once in every asset that mentions a number.
3. Describe 3:1 collateral as a "targeted collateral coverage ratio". Collateral value depends on case outcomes, collections, and enforceability, and may be less than expected.
4. No past-performance claims unless counsel has approved the exact figures, time period, and methodology, and they are backed by records. Placeholders appear as [PERFORMANCE DATA - COUNSEL APPROVAL REQUIRED]. Default is to omit.
5. No testimonials or third-party endorsements without counsel approval.
6. No claims that the offering is "SEC approved", "registered", or "endorsed". Securities are unregistered, restricted, and illiquid.
7. Do not describe any specific medical provider, law firm, or patient by name. Do not share any PHI (HIPAA). Receivable data stays in the data room, post-verification.
8. Keep outreach to an invitation to learn more. The offering terms live only in the PPM/subscription documents. Direct anyone who wants specifics to the PPM.
9. Do not tailor messages by guessing wealth or net worth. Verification (Section 6) is the gate; "accredited" is never assumed.

**506(c) specifics**
- General solicitation is allowed under 506(c), but every purchaser must be verified accredited via reasonable steps (third-party letter from a registered broker-dealer, RIA, licensed attorney, or CPA; tax documents; bank/brokerage statements plus credit report; or a qualifying written verification service). Self-certification alone is not enough.
- Do not accept funds or send subscription documents before verification is complete and documented.
- Confirm Form D (and California notice filing under Section 25102.1(d)) status and "bad actor" (Rule 506(d)) diligence on all covered persons before any outreach goes live.
- Every written asset should carry the standard legend (Appendix A) once approved.

**Referral partner compensation (HIGH RISK)**
- Paying transaction-based compensation (commissions, finder's fees tied to investments) to anyone not registered as a broker-dealer may violate federal and state law. RIAs and their reps may have additional fiduciary, disclosure, and Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1) obligations. CPAs and attorneys have professional-conduct and conflict-disclosure obligations.
- Default stance in these templates: **no referral compensation is offered or implied.** If compensation is ever contemplated, it must go through a registered broker-dealer under a written agreement approved by counsel, with disclosure to investors. [ATTORNEY TO APPROVE]
- Partners must not forward PFD materials as their own advice. They may introduce PFD; they do not solicit or recommend on PFD's behalf.

**Email (CAN-SPAM + California)**
- Accurate From/Reply-To and subject lines. No deceptive subjects.
- Identify the message as an advertisement/solicitation where applicable.
- Include a valid physical postal address and a working opt-out mechanism, honored within 10 business days (target: same day). Opt-out must work for at least 30 days after sending. No fee or login to opt out.
- Maintain a suppression list; never re-add opted-out contacts. Scrub every send against it.
- Keep records of opt-in source, date, and IP/consent text for the direct-investor sequence.
- California residents: honor CCPA/CPRA requests (opt-out of sale/sharing, deletion); link the privacy policy.
- Standard footer (use in every email): see Appendix B.

**Phone, voicemail and text**
- Scrub every call list against the **National Do Not Call Registry** and the **California Do Not Call list** and PFD's internal DNC list before each campaign (federal rule: scrub at least every 31 days). Also scrub against other states' DNC lists for out-of-state prospects.
- Calling hours: no telemarketing calls before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. **in the recipient's local time** (federal TSR/TCPA). California recommended practice: call only between 9:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m. recipient local time, and confirm current California and any other state rules with counsel (some states are stricter and restrict weekends/holidays). [ATTORNEY TO APPROVE]
- Identify yourself, PFD, and the purpose at the start. Honor "do not call" immediately, log it, and add the number to the internal DNC list the same day.
- Display a callable number on caller ID. No spoofing.
- **Call recording: California is an all-party consent state (Penal Code 632).** Do not record or monitor without telling the person and getting a yes. Disclose at the start of every call if recording.
- Established business relationship and "prior express invitation or permission" exceptions are narrow. Do not rely on them without counsel sign-off. Calls to people who opted in on a web form are permitted only if the consent language covered phone contact and the number is still not on a DNC list that overrides it per counsel's guidance.
- Autodialer, prerecorded, or AI-voice calls and **text messages** require **prior express written consent** (TCPA) that names PFD, covers marketing texts/calls, includes the number, and is not a condition of anything. Do not buy lists and text them. Do not text referral-partner or investor numbers without documented consent.
- All outreach is business solicitation to an individual's personal capacity as an investor and should be treated as consumer marketing for TCPA purposes unless counsel says otherwise.

**LinkedIn**
- Follow LinkedIn's User Agreement and Professional Community Policies (no automation tools, no scraping, no misleading connection notes).
- Messaging a 1st-degree connection about an offering is still a securities solicitation. Use approved language only. Do not discuss terms in public posts or comments without legend and counsel approval (a public post is general solicitation and must be 506(c) compliant).
- RIA-registered partners may be restricted by their firm's social media policy. Respect it.

**Recordkeeping**
- Log every touch (date, channel, template ID, version, sender) in the CRM. Retain copies of approved templates and all communications for at least the period counsel specifies (suggest 5+ years).

## 3. Phone call script

**Pre-call checklist (every call)**
- [ ] Number scrubbed against National DNC, California DNC, other relevant state DNC, and internal DNC (within last 31 days).
- [ ] Prospect's local time is 8:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. (California practice: 9:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m.; avoid holidays). 
- [ ] Prospect opted in or is a business contact whose number is permitted for this purpose (counsel-approved basis noted in CRM).
- [ ] Not recording unless disclosure + consent is obtained.
- [ ] Caller ID shows a valid PFD number.

**3.1 Opening**
> "Hi, may I speak with {{first_name}}? ... This is {{your name}} with PFD Capital Partners in Irvine. I'm calling because you {{requested information on our private offering on [date] / we connected on LinkedIn}}. Is now a good time for a few minutes? ... Just so you know, this call {{is not recorded / may be recorded; is that okay?}}."

If "not a good time": ask for a better time within calling hours; end.
If "don't call me": "Understood. I'll add you to our do-not-call list right away. Sorry for the interruption." End, log immediately, add to internal DNC.

**3.2 Frame (30 seconds)**
> "Quick overview so you know what this is: we run a private offering for verified accredited investors that funds medical receivables for doctors who treat personal-injury patients. The minimum is $100,000. We target roughly 15% annually, but that's a target, not a promise, and investors can lose money. It's also illiquid. Does that sound like something worth a few minutes?"

**3.3 Discovery questions** (listen more than talk; do not pressure; do not suggest suitability)
1. What prompted you to look at private credit or alternatives?
2. Have you invested in private placements before? Which kinds?
3. How do you think about illiquid investments in your overall portfolio? What share of your investable assets would you be comfortable having locked up for a multi-year period?
4. What time horizon and need for liquidity do you have? Any upcoming major expenses?
5. Do you work with a CPA, attorney, or advisor you'd want to review this with? (Encourage this.)
6. What would you need to see or understand to feel comfortable evaluating something like this?
7. Are you familiar with accredited-investor requirements? (Do not ask for net worth numbers on this call. Verification is handled separately.)

Disqualify politely if: they cannot be verified accredited, need liquidity, are looking for "guaranteed" returns, or say they would be investing money they cannot afford to lose.

**3.4 Objection handling**

*Do not argue. Acknowledge, answer honestly, and point to the PPM. Never soften risk.*

**"What if cases don't settle?"**
> "That's the central risk, and it's a fair question. When a case settles late, for less than expected, or doesn't settle, the receivable may be paid late, partially, or not at all. We try to manage that through underwriting, diversification across receivables, and a targeted 3:1 collateral coverage. But that ratio is a target, collateral may be worth less or take longer to collect, and you can lose some or all of your principal. The PPM goes through this in detail in the risk factors, and I'd encourage you to read those first."

**"Why 15%?"**
> "It's a target based on how we price the funding we provide. It's not a prediction and not a guarantee. Actual returns could be lower, delayed, or negative. Higher targeted returns generally reflect higher risk: illiquidity, case-outcome risk, and concentration. The PPM explains how returns are intended to be generated and the fees and expenses that reduce them." [Insert approved fee/expense disclosure - COUNSEL]

**"What about liquidity? Can I get out early?"**
> "Assume you can't. It's an illiquid private placement. Securities are restricted and there is no public market. {{Insert approved description of term, distribution timing, and any redemption provisions from the PPM, and say there is no assurance of redemption}}. You should only consider money you won't need during the term."

**"Is this safe? Is my principal protected?"**
> "No investment of this kind is safe in the sense of guaranteed principal. Your principal is at risk. The collateral is meant to be a cushion, not a promise."

**"How do you know the doctors/lawyers are legitimate?"**
> "Diligence is part of our process. Without describing specifics on the phone, I can tell you what's in the PPM and what we'd share in the data room after verification. I'd rather you review the documents than rely on my summary."

**"What's your track record?"**
> Default: "I'd rather point you to the PPM than quote numbers on a phone call. Past performance isn't a guarantee of future results." Only quote performance if counsel has approved [PERFORMANCE DATA - COUNSEL APPROVAL REQUIRED].

**"Is this legal/SEC approved? Are PI medical liens ethical?"**
> "It's offered under SEC Rule 506(c), which does not involve SEC approval or review of the merits. Securities regulators haven't approved the offering. I'm happy to send the PPM so your attorney can review the legal and regulatory risks."

**"What are the fees?"**
> "All fees and expenses are in the PPM. They reduce investor returns. I'll send the exact section."

**"I need to think about it / talk to my advisor."**
> "Absolutely. I'd encourage that. Can I send the overview and PPM to your email? I'll only follow up if you'd like."

**"Take me off your list."**
> "Of course. I'm removing you now. Sorry for the bother." (Log DNC immediately. No further contact. No "just one more thing.")

**3.5 Hand-off to verification (only if the prospect wants to proceed)**
> "If you'd like to move forward, the next step is verifying your accredited status. SEC rules require that for this type of offering, and we can't take any investment until that's done. You have three options: a letter from your CPA, attorney, RIA or broker-dealer; tax documents or account statements with a credit report; or a third-party verification service. I'll email you a secure link and the PPM. I'm not asking you to commit to anything today. After verification, our compliance team will follow up, and you'll receive the subscription documents. Hand-off protocol:
1. Do not collect SSN, account numbers, tax returns, or financial statements by phone or regular email. Use the secure portal only.
2. Create CRM record: consent source, call date/time, notes, materials sent (PPM version).
3. Send the PPM + risk factors + verification instructions email (template in compliance folder).
4. Notify compliance/verification lead. No subscription docs or wire instructions go out until verification is complete and approved.
5. Never mark a lead "qualified" based on what the prospect says verbally.

**3.6 Close**
