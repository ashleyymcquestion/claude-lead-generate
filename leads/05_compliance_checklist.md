# PFD Capital Partners: Rule 506(c) Compliance Checklist

**IMPORTANT: This is a checklist for discussion with securities counsel. It is not legal advice.** Rules, thresholds, and fees change; confirm every item (including citations and current fees) with qualified securities counsel before relying on it or launching any outreach. Nothing here should be used to contact a prospect until counsel has approved the offering structure and materials.

**Offering snapshot:** PFD Capital Partners (Irvine, CA). Rule 506(c) (Reg D) raise from accredited investors only. Minimum $100,000. Funds medical receivables for personal-injury doctors. Target 3:1 collateral coverage. Targeted return ~15% (a target, not a promise).

## 1. Structure and Threshold Questions for Counsel

- [ ] Confirm the issuer entity (fund/SPV/LLC), its manager, and that the interests are "securities" (assume yes: investment contract/note).
- [ ] Confirm 506(c) is the right exemption versus 506(b) (506(b) bars general solicitation but allows up to 35 non-accredited sophisticated investors and self-certification; 506(c) permits general solicitation but requires verification of every purchaser).
- [ ] Decide whether the vehicle is a pooled fund (Investment Company Act 3(c)(1)/3(c)(7) analysis) or a direct note/participation program. Counsel to opine on Investment Company Act and Advisers Act status of the issuer/manager.
- [ ] Confirm whether the activity of funding/purchasing medical receivables triggers any California lender/finance licensing (CFL), factoring, or related regulation, and any HIPAA exposure when handling patient-level receivable data.
- [ ] Confirm that all purchasers will be accredited, no exceptions (one non-accredited purchaser destroys 506(c)).

## 2. Accredited Investor Verification (Rule 506(c)(2)(ii))

The issuer must take **reasonable steps to verify**; checking a box is not enough. Document the method used for every investor before accepting funds.

- [ ] Adopt a written verification policy (who verifies, what evidence, how long it's valid, who approves).
- [ ] Decide a principles-based vs. safe-harbor approach per investor. Common methods (confirm current SEC text with counsel):
  - [ ] **Income (natural persons):** IRS forms (W-2, 1040, 1099, K-1) for the two most recent years plus written representation of expected current-year income; thresholds $200k individual / $300k joint.
  - [ ] **Net worth:** Statements dated within last 3 months (bank, brokerage, retirement) and a credit report within 3 months, plus written representation; net worth over $1M excluding primary residence.
  - [ ] **Third-party letter:** Written confirmation within the prior 3 months from a registered broker-dealer, SEC-registered investment adviser, licensed attorney, or CPA that they verified accredited status.
  - [ ] **Entities:** Entity-type analysis (e.g., $5M total assets entity, or all equity owners accredited); financial statements, formation docs, look-through verification where applicable.
  - [ ] **Licensed professional (Series 7/65/82) in good standing:** verify via FINRA BrokerCheck, if counsel agrees this category applies.
  - [ ] **Existing investors:** Counsel to confirm the minimum-investment-amount path under SEC guidance (high-minimum, cash investors with written representations); the $100k minimum alone is likely NOT sufficient on its own. Do not rely on it without counsel sign-off.
- [ ] Use a third-party verification service if desired (cost, privacy, and data-security review of vendor).
- [ ] Re-verify/refresh when a previous verification is stale (define a validity window, commonly 90 days).
- [ ] Obtain written representations in the subscription agreement that investor is accredited and is investing for own account.
- [ ] Prohibit closing/accepting funds until verification is complete and filed in the investor record.
- [ ] Protect verification documents (sensitive financial data): encrypted storage, access limits, retention policy.
- [ ] Red flags that require escalation: investor refuses verification, funds come from a third party, inconsistent documents, or pressure to "skip the paperwork." Never "wink" an investor through.

## 3. General Solicitation Rules (506(c))

General solicitation (public ads, social posts, webinars, podcasts, mass email, cold outreach, paid lead lists) IS permitted under 506(c), but:

- [ ] All sales must be only to verified accredited investors.
- [ ] Every public statement is an offering communication subject to anti-fraud liability (see Section 9).
- [ ] Consider a standard legend on all public materials: offering made only to accredited investors by PPM; not an offer in any state where unlawful; past performance/targets not guaranteed; investing involves risk including loss of principal. (Counsel to approve exact text.)
- [ ] Keep a master log of every public communication (date, channel, text, approver). See Recordkeeping.
- [ ] Do not allow unapproved employees, doctors, or affiliates to post, comment, or speak about the offering.
- [ ] Confirm general solicitation in other countries (if any foreign investors) with counsel; Reg S and foreign law considerations.
- [ ] If a previous private (506(b)-style) outreach occurred, confirm pre-existing relationship facts and that switching to 506(c) is done properly (Form D box checked for 506(c)).
- [ ] State-level: confirm no state restricts the general solicitation methods being used.

## 4. Bad Actor Disqualification (Rule 506(d)) and Disclosure (Rule 506(e))

- [ ] Identify all "covered persons": the issuer; directors, executive officers, other officers who participate in the offering; general partners/managing members; 20% beneficial owners of voting equity; promoters; any compensated solicitor (placement agent/finder) and their directors, officers, GPs, managing members.
- [ ] Send a **bad actor questionnaire** to each covered person BEFORE first sale (and update at least annually and at each closing/Form D amendment). Questionnaire should cover (confirm lookback periods with counsel):
  - [ ] Criminal convictions (securities-related, fraud, false filings; typical 5-year lookback for issuer/10-year for others)
  - [ ] Court injunctions/restraining orders related to securities
  - [ ] Final orders of state securities, banking, insurance regulators, federal banking agencies, CFTC, or NCUA
  - [ ] SEC disciplinary orders (broker/adviser registration suspensions, bars)
  - [ ] SEC cease-and-desist orders (5-year lookback)
  - [ ] SRO suspensions/expulsions (e.g., FINRA)
  - [ ] Stop orders / Reg A suspensions
  - [ ] U.S. Postal Service false representation orders
  - [ ] Any pre-September 23, 2013 events (disclosure obligations under 506(e))
- [ ] Perform independent diligence: SEC litigation/enforcement search, FINRA BrokerCheck, IAPD, state (DFPI, other) actions, court dockets, news and Medical Board checks where the doctors' principals are involved. Do not rely solely on self-reporting.
- [ ] Require covered persons to notify PFD immediately of any change; document reliance on "reasonable care" exception.
- [ ] If any disqualifying event exists: stop; counsel to evaluate waiver (SEC), cure, or removal of person; 506(e) disclosure of pre-2013 events to investors.
- [ ] Retain completed questionnaires and diligence results with the offering file.

## 5. Form D (Rule 503)

- [ ] File Form D electronically via EDGAR **within 15 calendar days after the first sale** (date the first investor is irrevocably contractually committed, which could be signing a subscription agreement subject to acceptance, rather than funds clearing; counsel to determine the exact trigger).
- [ ] Obtain EDGAR access codes (Form ID, notarized; allow time, so start well before the first sale). Check current EDGAR Next requirements (account administrators/login.gov credentials) with counsel.
- [ ] If the 15th day falls on a weekend/holiday, file the next business day (confirm).
- [ ] Select Rule 506(c) on the form.
- [ ] Accurately list related persons, offering amount, sales compensation recipients (any finder/broker named here), and use of proceeds (percentage to related persons).
- [ ] File Form D amendments annually if the offering continues, and promptly for material changes (e.g., offering amount, new related persons).
- [ ] Calendar the filing deadline when the first subscription is accepted. Form D is not a condition of the exemption, but failure can trigger state penalties and SEC injunctions.
- [ ] Confirm consistency: Form D statements, PPM, and marketing must match.

## 6. California Blue Sky (Corp. Code 25102.1 / DFPI)

- [ ] Rule 506 interests are "covered securities" under NSMIA (Securities Act 18(b)(4)(F)), so California requires a notice filing, not a registration/qualification. Counsel to confirm the current state of the rule.
- [ ] File a **notice of the Rule 506 offering with the California DFPI** (copy of Form D + fee) via the NASAA EFD system, **within 15 days after the first sale in California**. Confirm that Corp. Code 25102.1(d) is the applicable provision and current fee/schedule.
- [ ] Repeat the notice filing for **every other state** where an investor resides (each state has its own deadline, fee, and form; most use EFD). Build a state-by-state tracker keyed to the investor's state of residence at time of sale.
- [ ] Confirm state anti-fraud provisions still apply (Corp. Code 25401/25501 for misstatements, and Corp. Code 25400 for manipulative conduct) even though registration is preempted. California's civil liability under 25501 can include rescission.
- [ ] Confirm DFPI position on bad-actor and general solicitation matters for offers to California residents.
- [ ] Confirm whether Rule 506 offering notices are required for sales made before California residents sign (sales are determined at the state level; counsel to confirm).
- [ ] Calendar annual renewals/amendments if state requires them.

## 7. Broker-Dealer, Finder, and Compensation Risk

Unregistered broker activity is a top enforcement issue and can give investors rescission rights (Exchange Act 29(b); Corp. Code 25210 in California).

- [ ] **No transaction-based compensation** (commissions, percent of funds raised, per-investor bounties, success fees) to anyone not registered as a broker-dealer with FINRA membership and a registered representative, and licensed in the investor's state.
- [ ] Do not use "finders," "introducers," lead generators, family offices, or doctors who get paid per investor unless counsel has approved the arrangement in writing. Even "referral fees" tied to closings are suspect.
- [ ] Permissible alternatives to explore with counsel: engage a registered placement agent/broker-dealer; hire lead-gen vendors paid flat fixed fees unrelated to investor success (counsel to confirm); in-house employees paid salary (not transaction-based) who limit their role.
- [ ] Review PFD personnel: anyone who solicits investors, negotiates terms, handles funds, or is compensated based on sales must be assessed. Consider the issuer's "Rule 3a4-1" safe harbor for associated persons of issuers with counsel.
- [ ] Written agreements with all marketing vendors that exclude investment advice, no investor-specific compensation, and require compliance with PFD's approved materials.
- [ ] Disclose any compensation to placement agents/finders in the PPM and Form D.
- [ ] Do not pay doctors (borrowers/counterparties) to refer their investor friends, and watch conflicts of interest where doctors receiving funding are also investors.
- [ ] Add to bad actor questionnaire (Section 4) any compensated solicitor.
- [ ] Investment adviser question: if anyone gives personalized advice about whether to invest in the fund generally, confirm no unregistered adviser activity.

## 8. Marketing and Advertising Rules of Thumb

Even though Rule 506(c) allows general solicitation, there are no exceptions to anti-fraud rules. Treat every ad, email, post, deck, and call script as if the SEC and a plaintiff's lawyer will read it.

- [ ] **No guarantees.** Returns are targeted/projected only; principal is at risk. Never imply safe, risk-free, or assured.
- [ ] **Balanced risk disclosure.** Any statement of potential return must be accompanied by prominent risks in the same piece (illiquidity, loss of principal, concentration, litigation/case-outcome and collection risk, doctor/provider default, reliance on personal-injury case settlement timing, regulatory change, lien-priority disputes, conflicts of interest, fees to manager).
- [ ] **Substantiate the 3:1 collateral claim.** Be able to show, in writing, for each pool: definition of collateral (what is counted: face value of receivables? expected collectible value?), how valued, who verifies, what happens when coverage falls, lien perfection (UCC filings), priority versus other creditors, and the date measured. Say "target collateral coverage of 3:1 based on [defined measure]," not "backed 3x." Update the figure; stale claims are misleading.
- [ ] **Substantiate the ~15% target.** Maintain a model with assumptions (collection rates, timing, defaults, fees, expenses). State whether gross or net to investors, that it is a target not a forecast, and show realized track record only if it complies (full, representative, not cherry-picked). Be careful with hypothetical or projected performance.
- [ ] Any track record or testimonials: must be accurate, complete, representative; disclose whether testimonial authors are paid or are affiliates; obtain consent; keep supporting records. Consider Marketing Rule (Advisers Act 206(4)-1) guidance if applicable to PFD as an adviser.
- [ ] Do not describe the offering as FDIC/SIPC insured, "like a CD/bond," or "low risk."
- [ ] Every marketing piece must carry the approved legend and be reviewed and logged (approval date, approver, version) before use.
- [ ] Align with PPM: marketing must never contradict or go beyond the PPM. If the PPM changes, retire old materials.
- [ ] Fair statements about doctors/patients: no patient identifying data (HIPAA) in marketing; no misleading claims about medical-legal case outcomes.
- [ ] Social/influencer content: must be approved; disclose compensation; no comment-thread replies with return promises; monitor and delete unauthorized posts.
- [ ] Hold a script for phone calls and one-on-one meetings; no ad-libbed return/safety statements.

### Red-Flag Phrases to Avoid

Do not use (or allow anyone to use) language like the following without counsel review:

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Guaranteed return" / "guaranteed 15%" | Guarantee, false assurance |
| "Risk-free" / "safe" / "no risk" / "can't lose" | Misleading, omits risk |
| "Fully secured" / "fully backed" / "protected by 3x collateral" | Overstates collateral protection; collateral can fail |
| "Your principal is protected/insured" | Unsupported; no insurance |
| "Insurance-backed" / "lawyer-backed" / "settlement-backed" (unless true and explained) | Case outcomes are uncertain |
| "Fixed 15% return" / "earn 15% a year" (without "target") | Presented as certain |
| "Like a bond / CD / savings account" | False comparability |
| "Never lost money" / "perfect track record" / "no defaults ever" | Unsubstantiated absolute claims |
| "Limited spots, act now / last chance / closing tomorrow" | Pressure; can look manipulative |
| "SEC approved" / "SEC registered" / "government approved offering" | Form D filing is not approval |
| "Passive income for life" / "set it and forget it" | Hype, omits risk and illiquidity |
| "Better than the stock market" | Unsupported comparison |
| "Recession-proof" / "uncorrelated" | Unsubstantiated |
| "Liquid" / "withdraw anytime" (if untrue) | Illiquid security |
| "Tax-free" / "tax-advantaged" | Tax advice; unsubstantiated |
| "Trust me" / "I personally guarantee" | Personal assurances create liability |
| "Everyone qualifies" / "no verification needed" / "just check the box" | Defeats 506(c) |
| "Doctors always pay" / "insurance always pays" | Collection risk |
| "Anyone can invest" (public ads) | Must state accredited only |

Safer framing examples (counsel to approve): "targeted net return of approximately 15% (not guaranteed)", "target collateral coverage of 3:1 as defined in the PPM", "investing involves risk, including loss of all or part of your investment", "available only to verified accredited investors, via PPM."

## 9. Anti-Fraud (Rule 10b-5, Securities Act 17(a), California Corp. Code 25401)

- [ ] No untrue statement of material fact; no omission of a material fact needed to make statements not misleading, in the PPM, deck, website, social posts, emails, and oral statements.
- [ ] Material facts to confirm disclosed: use of proceeds; fees, carry, and affiliate compensation; related-party transactions with doctors/law firms/funders; concentration in a few providers or lawyers; historical write-offs and delays; litigation/regulatory actions; lien priority; leverage; how receivables are purchased and priced; how collateral is monitored; conflicts of interest.
- [ ] Verification process for factual claims: source documents for each number used in marketing (a "claims substantiation file").
- [ ] Designate a compliance owner; second-person review on marketing and investor reports.
- [ ] Ongoing duty: update investors on material developments (defaults, case losses, regulatory inquiries), and correct previously made statements.
- [ ] Keep promotional communications consistent over time (no changing story between investors).
- [ ] Escalate and document any investor complaints or questions on risk.
- [ ] Potential liability: SEC enforcement, state DFPI action, investor rescission/damages, and personal liability for control persons. Counsel to advise on insurance (D&O/E&O) and indemnities.

## 10. Offering Documents

- [ ] **Private Placement Memorandum (PPM):** business description, management, risk factors (extensive), terms, use of proceeds, fees and expenses, conflicts, collateral and servicing description, redemption/liquidity terms, tax considerations, ERISA (if retirement accounts), legal proceedings, bad-actor statement, financial information, forward-looking statement cautions.
- [ ] **Operating agreement / note indenture / LPA** as applicable.
- [ ] **Subscription agreement:** accredited representations, investor questionnaire, anti-money-laundering representations, acknowledgement of risk and illiquidity, restrictions on transfer, state legends, W-9/W-8, wire instructions, and consent to e-delivery.
- [ ] **Investor questionnaire** with verification evidence checklist (links to Section 2).
- [ ] Legends: restricted securities (Rule 144), not registered under the 1933 Act or state laws, and required California legend language as advised by counsel.
- [ ] Escrow/payment handling: segregated account; funds not released until conditions are met; confirm with counsel whether a third-party escrow is needed.
- [ ] AML/KYC: OFAC screening of investors; evaluate whether PFD needs an AML program; wire fraud protocol (verify wire instructions by phone).
- [ ] Data room with receivable-level information must exclude patient PHI or be de-identified; confirm HIPAA/business associate considerations and California CMIA.
- [ ] Ongoing investor reporting standards and form.
- [ ] Tax: K-1 or 1099 handling; counsel/CPA review.

## 11. Outreach Compliance: TCPA, DNC, CAN-SPAM, CCPA

(Applies to the lead-generation activity in the rest of this folder. Counsel to review before any outbound campaign.)

**TCPA (calls/texts)**
- [ ] No autodialed or prerecorded/AI-voice calls or texts to mobile numbers without prior express written consent. Treat all texting as consent-required.
- [ ] Respect calling hours: 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time.
- [ ] Keep a written consent record (date, source, wording) per contact (see `crm_tracker.csv` `consent_to_contact`).
- [ ] Honor revocation of consent promptly, including by informal means (e.g., "stop," "don't contact me").
- [ ] Maintain an internal do-not-call list.

**Do Not Call (federal and California)**
- [ ] Scrub phone lists against the National DNC Registry before cold calls (use a registered SAN, ~31-day scrub cycle) and against the PFD internal DNC list. Confirm whether a B2B or "established business relationship" exception applies (counsel to confirm; do not assume).
- [ ] Confirm California DNC rules and any state "mini-TCPA" laws (e.g., Florida, Oklahoma, Maryland) for investors in those states.
- [ ] Record the scrub date in `dnc_checked`.
- [ ] Identify caller and company at the start of calls; provide a number/address; recorded-call consent rules: California is an all-party consent state (Penal Code 632), so no recording without consent.
- [ ] Telemarketing sales rule disclosures where applicable.

**CAN-SPAM (email)**
- [ ] Accurate "From," reply-to, and subject lines; no deception.
- [ ] Identify the message as an advertisement where required; include a valid physical postal address (PFD Irvine address).
- [ ] Clear, working opt-out mechanism; honor within 10 business days (target immediately); never charge or require login to opt out.
- [ ] Do not sell/transfer opt-out lists; maintain suppression list.
- [ ] Responsible for vendor-sent email too. Cold email to business addresses is permitted under CAN-SPAM if compliant (note: California B&P 17529.5 and other states may add restrictions; counsel to confirm).
- [ ] Do not scrape or buy lists whose source cannot be documented. Record the source of each lead (`source` column).

**CCPA/CPRA (California privacy)**
- [ ] Determine whether PFD meets CCPA thresholds (revenue, volume of consumers' data, or revenue from selling/sharing personal info). Counsel to determine; contact data of individuals and B2B contacts can be covered.
- [ ] If applicable: privacy policy on website, notice at collection when gathering data, honoring access/delete/correct/opt-out (sale/share) requests within statutory time limits, and service-provider contracts with vendors.
- [ ] Data minimization: collect only what is needed; do not keep sensitive data (SSN, full financial statements) in the CRM; keep verification documents in secure storage only.
- [ ] Set retention periods; delete or suppress data of people who ask or who never engage.
- [ ] GLBA/Reg S-P considerations for investor financial data: counsel to evaluate; at minimum maintain reasonable security.
- [ ] Data breach response plan (California Civil Code 1798.82 notification).

## 12. Recordkeeping

- [ ] Keep (suggested retention at least 6 years after final closing; counsel to set; some states/rules may require longer):
  - [ ] Offering documents (every version, with dates of use)
  - [ ] Master log of every general solicitation and marketing communication, with approval records and distribution lists
  - [ ] Claims substantiation file (3:1 collateral calculations, return model, track record data)
  - [ ] Accredited investor verification evidence and approval record for each investor
  - [ ] Signed subscription agreements and investor questionnaires
  - [ ] Bad actor questionnaires, diligence results, and update records
  - [ ] Form D and amendments; state notice filings, fees, and confirmations
  - [ ] Finder/vendor/placement agreements and payment records
  - [ ] Consent, DNC scrubs, opt-out and suppression logs; privacy requests
  - [ ] Investor communications and complaints
  - [ ] Fund accounting, collateral monitoring and reports to investors
  - [ ] Call/meeting notes (CRM) stored in a consistent, factual, non-promissory manner (avoid writing speculative or promissory language in notes)
- [ ] Back up data; restrict access; keep audit trail of CRM changes.
- [ ] Litigation hold procedure if a complaint or inquiry arises.
